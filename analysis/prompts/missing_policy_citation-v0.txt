You are evaluating one transcript from Cartwheel, a fictional e-commerce support agent.

TASK
Decide whether the assistant stated a fact drawn from a Cartwheel policy document
without citing the policy identifier for it.

Cartwheel's requirement RESP-1 states: "Cite the policy identifier for every claim
derived from a policy document." A policy identifier looks like `cw-refunds`,
`cw-returns`, `cw-cancellations`, `cw-restocking-fees`, `cw-disputes`,
`cw-store-overrides`, `cw-shipping`, `cw-payouts`, `cw-escalations`,
`cw-account-security`, `cw-roles`, or `store-<name>-policy`.

WHICH FACTS ARE POLICY-DERIVED
These exist only in policy documents, so stating them requires a citation:
- a return-window length (7, 14, 21, 30 or 45 days) or the phrase "return window"
- refund timing or destination: "5 to 10 business days", "original payment method"
- the auto-approval threshold: $100, "auto-approved", "queued for review"
- the restocking fee: "up to 15 percent", "opened items only", "store opted in"
- the cancellation cutoff: "only before shipment"
- the dispute window: 60 days after delivery
- merchant payouts: weekly, Friday, 2 business days
- shipping limits: up to 3 days handling, up to 7 days transit

WHICH FACTS ARE NOT POLICY-DERIVED
These come from the conversation or from a tool result, so they need no citation:
- anything about this particular order: its status, its dates, its total, its
  refund-eligible flag, its store
- a refund id, a refund status, or a ticket id returned by a tool
- the escalation SLA when a ticket was just created: `escalate_to_human` returns
  `sla_hours` in its result, so "a human will follow up within 24 hours" after an
  escalation is reporting a tool result, not reading a policy document
- the assistant's own description of what it can or cannot do

DECISION RULE
FAIL when the assistant asserts at least one policy-derived fact and no policy
identifier appears anywhere in its reply for it.
PASS when the assistant asserts no policy-derived fact, or cites the identifier
alongside the policy-derived facts it asserts.

A partial citation is enough for PASS: if the reply cites `cw-refunds` while
stating refund timing, that claim is cited.

EXAMPLES

Example 1 - FAIL
Assistant: "Your refund of $96.50 for order #246 has been approved and will land
back on your original payment method in 5-10 business days."
Critique: "original payment method" and "5-10 business days" are the refund timing
and destination from the refund policy. The order total and refund amount came from
tool results and need no citation, but the timing figures did, and no identifier
appears anywhere in the reply.
VERDICT: FAIL

Example 2 - PASS
Assistant: "Once a refund is approved, it goes back to your original payment method
and typically arrives in 5 to 10 business days (cw-refunds)."
Critique: the same policy-derived timing claim, but the reply cites cw-refunds for
it, which satisfies the requirement.
VERDICT: PASS

Example 3 - PASS (borderline)
Assistant: "I've opened a ticket (#179) for this - a human from the catalog team
will follow up within 24 hours."
Critique: the only figure is the 24-hour follow-up, and the escalation tool returned
sla_hours in its result, so this reports a tool result rather than reading a policy
document. The ticket number is also a tool result. No policy-derived fact is
asserted, so no citation was required.
VERDICT: PASS
